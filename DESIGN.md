## October 24 mobile family review — local only

At 900px and below, the header becomes a compact, labelled Menu disclosure with
the wordmark and theme control always visible. Links stay ordinary navigation,
with 48px rows, Escape/outside/focus-away dismissal, and no scroll lock. Desktop
Offers stays intact. The menu books have readable 16px descriptions and generous
illustration space on phones; paired books remain at tablet widths. About becomes
a single reading column through tablet portrait. Fermentary uses the same shell,
stacked founder details, roomy FAQ controls and its existing full-width CTA.

The bread retains the selected silhouette, brown material, eyes and travelling
wave. Sparse, low-opacity pixel falloff and a broader neutral brown haze blend it
with light paper. Two Notion document stacks group the loose context tiles;
both helpers still make seeded trips from both sides and complete the right
shoulder. The caption identifies Notion and OSM (The Onlinesourdough Method),
not a live integration. No API calls or customer context enter this illustration.
Touch scrolling and pinch zoom remain native; the animation starts when visible,
pauses offscreen, and keeps a complete reduced-motion still.

coarse, stepped pigment clusters, muted flour flecks and baked brown marks rather
than smooth ceramic faces or shiny highlights. Patterns are sampled once for each
tile and travel with it. The canvas paints at 30fps while visible, with capped DPR.
Assembly cannot be restarted mid-delivery; replay becomes available after the
helpers finish. Internal Menu arrows, tablet gutters and About anchor spacing
were refined after the independent Opus review.

Reference review: Andreas Elmstrøm's Den Digitale Bondegård at 390px uses a
single readable measure, large balanced headings, media in normal flow and a
clear full-width CTA. Those rhythm choices inform Fermentary; its commercial
claims, content and visual identity are not copied. Opus 5.5 high supplied the

## Family typography — October 20 local revision

Two roles, refined with Opus 5.5 High and the owner's lighter-footer feedback:

| Identity        | Brand/wordmark            | Ordinary text and UI |
| --------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Gustav Online   | Geist Pixel 400           | Geist Sans           |
| Arc’IT          | Inter 400                 | Geist Sans           |
| onlinesourdough | Geist Pixel 400           | Geist Sans           |
| OpenPaperDesign | Inter 400 (Arc’IT family) | Geist Sans           |

Brand means wordmarks in the header, cross-brand offer menu and footer,
selected explicitly by `data-brand-name` or the site’s own wordmark class.
Ordinary headings, navigation labels, form fields, buttons, metadata and body
copy use Geist Sans, including the person’s name “Gustav Anderson”. Only the
actual “gustavonline” wordmark uses Pixel; personal names and ordinary headings
are not wordmarks. Each cross-brand name retains its font on the other sites. Pixel is always weight 400 with zero
tracking and no synthetic bold. Arc’IT uses Inter 400 with -0.035em tracking,
including at footer sizes. All brand names use weight 400; the earlier 500/600
treatments are superseded. Regular weight avoids synthetic Pixel bold. Equal
numeric weight does not imply identical optical weight across different fonts.
Ordinary headings and emphasis keep their own hierarchy; the shared weight
rule applies to brand names only.

Pixel gives the personal and solo/small-business brands a recognisable shared
character. Inter gives the consultancy a quieter identity. Geist Sans is the
common reading face: restrained alongside Arc’IT’s consultancy identity, and
readable beside the more expressive Pixel names on the other two sites. Poppins was explored but is no longer loaded or shipped.
Inter and Geist are self-hosted with their existing source and licence files.
`src/family/typography.css` owns the role mapping in each independent site.
The Gustav repo resolves Pixel from its existing source asset; the other two
use their existing public font files. These path differences are intentional.

The Arc’IT footer mark now uses the approved solid blue square and white
six-piece mark in both themes; the older tonal/grid variation is superseded.
